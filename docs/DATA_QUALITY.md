# Synthetic Data Quality Metrics

This document explains the data quality metrics implemented in the `metrics/data_quality.py` module and their theoretical relationships to model collapse.

## Overview

Model collapse occurs when an AI model is trained on synthetic data generated by other AI models, leading to a progressive loss of the original distribution's diversity. Over generations, the model "forgets" rare events and the output distribution narrows significantly.

By analyzing the properties of the synthetic data before training, we can detect early warning signs of collapse. We quantify these properties using the following metrics.

---

## 1. N-Gram Diversity
**Metric:** `ngram_diversity(data, max_n=5)`

### Description
Computes the ratio of unique n-grams (subsequences of length *n*) to the total number of possible n-grams in the dataset.

### Relationship to Model Collapse
Generative models exhibiting collapse tend to fall into repetitive loops or favor common, highly-probable phrases over diverse, rare ones. A drop in n-gram diversity—especially for higher values of *n*—is a strong leading indicator that the synthetic generator's output distribution is narrowing.

---

## 2. Token Distribution Shift
**Metric:** `token_distribution_shift(original_data, synthetic_data)`

### Description
Computes the Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence between the token frequency distributions of the original training data and the synthetic data.

### Relationship to Model Collapse
Model collapse mathematically manifests as a shift in the marginal probability of tokens, where low-probability tokens are systematically suppressed and high-probability tokens are amplified. A high KL divergence from the original dataset indicates severe distortion and impending collapse.

---

## 3. Sequence Length Comparison
**Metric:** `sequence_length_comparison(original_data, synthetic_data)`

### Description
Compares the distribution of sequence lengths between the original and synthetic datasets using:
1. **Kolmogorov-Smirnov (KS) Statistic:** Measures the maximum difference between the empirical cumulative distribution functions (CDFs).
2. **Wasserstein Distance:** Measures the minimum "cost" required to transform the synthetic length distribution into the original distribution.

### Relationship to Model Collapse
Collapsed models often produce truncated outputs or excessively long repetitive loops. If the synthetic dataset's length distribution significantly deviates from the original (high KS statistic or Wasserstein distance), the generator is no longer matching the structural bounds of the true distribution.

---

## 4. Memorization Detection
**Metric:** `memorization_detection(train_data, synthetic_data)`

### Description
Calculates the fraction of sequences in the synthetic dataset that exactly match sequences present in the original training set.

### Relationship to Model Collapse
While models should generalize, an over-reliance on exact memorization (verbatim replication) in synthetic data implies the generator has lost the ability to creatively interpolate within the manifold. High memorization rates suggest the model will merely reinforce existing training examples rather than providing novel, diverse samples, thus accelerating collapse.

---

## 5. Diversity Metrics
**Metric:** `diversity_metrics(data)`

### Description
Computes a suite of diversity indicators:
1. **Unique Sequence Fraction:** The percentage of sequences that are entirely unique.
2. **Type-Token Ratio (TTR):** The ratio of unique vocabulary tokens to the total number of tokens.
3. **Normalized Compression Distance (NCD):** An algorithmic information theory metric utilizing Zlib compression to measure the informational distance (similarity) between pairs of sequences.

### Relationship to Model Collapse
- **Unique Sequence Fraction:** A drop indicates the generator is producing the exact same sequences repeatedly.
- **TTR:** A declining TTR indicates lexical impoverishment.
- **NCD:** If the mean NCD across random pairs of sequences is low, it means the sequences share a high amount of structural information (they are highly similar). High NCD indicates high internal dataset diversity. A collapsed model generates highly compressible, structurally homogeneous outputs, resulting in lower NCD scores.
