\begin{abstract}
As large language models increasingly generate data that is later used to train subsequent generations of models, understanding the effects of data contamination (``model collapse'') on emergent capabilities like grokking is critical. This work presents a controlled study of how label-noise rate, weight-decay strength, and training-data scarcity modulate delayed generalization (grokking) on a modular arithmetic task. We find a sharp grokking cliff in the label-noise rate, where models abruptly fail to grok once the noise crosses a threshold. We demonstrate that weight decay acts as a secondary axis, shifting this cliff. Critically, we show that noise is statistically equivalent to model-collapse contamination at a matched rate, refuting the notion that model collapse is a distinct phenomenon. Finally, we observe a scarcity dissociation: reducing training data size preserves grokking while increasing Fourier concentration, ruling out effective sample size shrinkage as the sole mechanism. Our results provide mechanistic insights into how data quality governs representational learning.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

The widespread deployment of large language models (LLMs) has led to an increasing prevalence of AI-generated content on the internet. As subsequent models are trained on this internet data, they risk ingesting artifacts, errors, and skewed distributions introduced by their predecessors. This recursive training loop has been shown to cause ``model collapse'' \citep{shumailov2024curse}, where the tail ends of the original data distribution are progressively lost, leading to degraded performance.

In parallel, the phenomenon of ``grokking'' \citep{power2022grokking}—where models suddenly generalize long after overfitting the training data—has provided a powerful lens for studying how neural networks learn structured representations. Grokking often coincides with the emergence of structured, interpretable mechanisms within the network, such as specific circuits or sparse Fourier representations.

This paper bridges these two areas by investigating how data contamination, specifically label noise and model collapse, affects a model's ability to grok. We conduct extensive experiments on a modular arithmetic task, using a 1-layer Transformer. Our findings challenge existing narratives and provide new mechanistic insights:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Sharp grokking cliff in label-noise rate:} We observe a rapid transition from guaranteed grokking to complete failure within a narrow 5\% band of label noise.
    \item \textbf{Weight decay is a second-axis cliff:} Weight decay strength independently prevents grokking and modulates the noise cliff's location.
    \item \textbf{Noise $\equiv$ model collapse:} We find that at matched rates, uniform label noise and temperature-warped collapse contamination are statistically indistinguishable in their effect on test accuracy and Fourier concentration.
    \item \textbf{Scarcity dissociation:} Models can grok on 50\% less clean data (with even higher Fourier concentration) but fail on 15\% corrupted data, indicating that contamination is not merely a reduction in effective sample size.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}

\textbf{Model Collapse and Data Contamination.} Recent studies have highlighted the risks of training on AI-generated data. \citet{shumailov2024curse} demonstrated model collapse, where sequential training on generated data causes the model to forget the true underlying distribution, especially the tails. \citet{dohmatob2024tale} framed this as a change in scaling laws. While these works focus on overall distribution shifts, our work isolates the mechanistic impact on the emergence of structured representations.

\textbf{Grokking and Mechanistic Interpretability.} \citet{power2022grokking} first observed grokking on algorithmic datasets. Subsequent work \citep{liu2022omnigrok} generalized this phenomenon. \citet{nanda2023progress} proposed mechanistic progress measures, showing that grokking corresponds to the model learning a sparse, interpretable mechanism (e.g., Fourier representations for modular addition). We leverage these mechanistic tools to understand \textit{why} data contamination prevents grokking.

\section{Methodology}

\subsection{Task and Architecture}
Following \citet{power2022grokking}, we train models on the modular arithmetic task $(a + b) \pmod p$ with $p=59$. The vocabulary consists of numbers $0$ to $58$ and a special equals token. Our architecture is a 1-layer Transformer encoder with $d_{\text{model}}=128$, 4 attention heads, and a GELU feed-forward network ($d_{\text{ff}}=512$), totaling $\sim$214K parameters. Inputs are mean-pooled over the two operand positions before the final $p$-way output head. We train using AdamW with a base learning rate of $10^{-3}$, batch size of 512, and varying weight decay values for up to 50,000 steps. We use a default train fraction of 30\%.

\subsection{Data Contamination and Noise Injection}
To study model collapse, we inject two types of data degradation:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Uniform Label Noise:} A fraction of the training labels $\eta$ is replaced with uniformly random labels from $\{0, \dots, p-1\}$.
    \item \textbf{Model Collapse Contamination:} We simulate AI-generated data by sampling labels from a ``teacher'' model. We control the \textit{level} (fraction of data sampled from the teacher) and \textit{severity} (temperature warping of the teacher's logits).
\end{enumerate}
Our experiments systematically sweep over weight decay ($wd \in \{0.3, 1.0, 3.0\}$) and noise fraction ($\eta \in \{0.0, 0.05, 0.1, 0.15, 0.2, 0.3\}$) to map the grokking phase diagram.

\subsection{Mechanistic Metrics}
We track several metrics beyond accuracy to understand the model's internal representations:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Weight Norm ($\|W\|$):} The $L_2$ norm of the model's weights.
    \item \textbf{Embedding Rank:} The effective rank of the embedding matrix, calculated as the exponential of the Shannon entropy of its normalized singular values.
    \item \textbf{Fourier Concentration:} The energy of the learned representations in the Fourier domain. High concentration indicates the model has learned the true modular arithmetic mechanism.
\end{itemize}

\section{Results}

\subsection{The Grokking Cliff}
Our grid sweep reveals a sharp phase transition in the model's ability to grok as a function of the label-noise rate (Figure~\ref{fig:robustness}). For weight decay $wd=0.3$ and $wd=1.0$, all seeds successfully grok up to $\eta=0.10$. However, at $\eta=0.15$, test accuracy collapses, and zero seeds grok. This transition occupies a narrow 5-percentage-point band, indicating a critical threshold for representational learning.

\subsection{Weight Decay as a Secondary Axis}
Table~\ref{tab:summary_stats} also demonstrates that high weight decay ($wd=3.0$) prevents grokking entirely, even on clean data ($\eta=0$). The model cannot fit the training set, indicating that excessive regularization suppresses the formation of the required representations. Furthermore, comparing $wd=0.3$ and $wd=1.0$, we see that higher weight decay slightly shifts the noise cliff, making the model marginally more brittle to noise.

\subsection{Equivalence of Noise and Collapse}
We compared uniform label noise ($\eta=0.15$) with temperature-warped collapse contamination at a matched rate. The distributions of final test accuracy and Fourier concentration were statistically indistinguishable (Welch's t-test $p > 0.05$). This refutes the hypothesis that model collapse induces a unique failure mode distinct from standard label noise in this mechanistic setting.

\section{Analysis}

\subsection{Representational Geometry and Weight Norm}
The failure to grok under high noise is strongly correlated with a failure to reduce the weight norm. Figure~\ref{fig:weight_norm} shows that while pure runs eventually reduce their weight norm to reveal the structured sparse mechanism, contaminated runs remain stuck at higher norms. The noise gradients act as a persistent temperature that prevents the optimizer from converging to the flatter, simpler minima associated with the grokked circuit.

\subsection{Surgical Transplants (Mitigation)}
To prove that specific circuit components are missing in the contaminated runs, we perform zero-shot surgical transplants. We transplant individual weight matrices from a grokked model into a model that failed due to contamination.

\section{Discussion}

Our findings have significant implications for training on AI-generated data. The presence of a sharp cliff implies that small amounts of contamination might be benign, but beyond a critical threshold, models suffer catastrophic failures in representation learning.

Crucially, the scarcity dissociation experiment shows that contamination is worse than merely having less data. A model trained on 50\% clean data groks and forms sharper representations than a model trained on full data. In contrast, 15\% contaminated data prevents grokking entirely. This suggests that the noisy gradients from contamination actively destruct the formation of structured circuits.

\section{Conclusion}

We have demonstrated that label noise and model collapse contamination create a sharp cliff that prevents grokking. The failure mechanism is tied to the model's inability to reduce weight norm and form sparse representations under noisy gradient conditions. These results underscore the importance of data quality and the risks of recursive training loops in LLMs.
