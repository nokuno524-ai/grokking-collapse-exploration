\begin{abstract}
This paper investigates how model collapse affects the grokking phenomenon in modular arithmetic transformers. We demonstrate that pure data results in grokking at step 1400, while severe collapse prevents grokking entirely. We additionally show that weight decay acts as a second-axis cliff.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Grokking, the phenomenon where a model transitions from overfitting to perfect generalization after extended training \cite{power2022grokking}, is well-studied in algorithmic datasets. Simultaneously, progress measures \cite{chan2023progress} have helped formalize this behavior mechanistically. Model collapse \cite{dohmatob2024tale} occurs when generative models are trained recursively on AI-generated data. This study bridges the gap, investigating how collapse severity impacts the delayed generalization of small transformers.
